sprint-forecast

hero

Prices a committed delivery date as a probability, not a promise.

The impossibilityA single committed delivery date cannot state its own probability of being met; only a distribution over the team's past throughput can.
  • 4 of 12 committed dates sat below P50, a coin flip sold as a promise
  • P85 covered 82.9% and P95 covered 90.8% on 2000 synthetic teams, a 4.1-point tail optimism
  • P95 coverage climbs from 83.8% at four sprints to 94.9% at forty

flow-metrics

Finds a slowdown weeks before the average cycle time admits it.

The impossibilityAn average cycle time cannot reveal a slowdown already building, because WIP can climb while the average stays flat.
  • WIP broke its band at week 13, the cycle-time average not until week 21, an 8-week lead flat for 6 of them
  • the reported average understated eventual cost by 68% at the worst week, 5.1 days reported against 16.1 eventual
  • Little's Law tracks within 5% in steady state and 57% in the transition, 11x wider

raid-signal

Turns a RAID log into a signal that knows normal churn from trouble.

The impossibilityA RAID log is a list; a raw open-risk count cannot say normal churn from trouble, because a count has no baseline.
  • SPC signalled in week 16, a 4-week lead over the manual escalation in week 20
  • the lower control limit clamped in 100% of weeks, blind to improvement
  • the 3-sigma breach led the run rules by 5 weeks on this spike

earned-value

hero

Separates under-budget from behind-schedule before the burn chart can.

The impossibilitySpend-to-date alone cannot tell under-budget from behind-schedule; they look identical on a burn chart until progress is earned separately.
  • 14 weeks where spend sat within 5% of budget while SPI was already below 0.95
  • worst concealed slip $73,506 at week 22; the program finished $339,975 over budget, 13.6%
  • SPI recovers to an exact 1.000 at completion while CPI never recovers, the tool's own blind spot

release-readiness

A go/no-go gate that a UAT pass-rate cannot fool.

The impossibilityA UAT pass-rate cannot tell you a release is safe; it weighs a trivial test like a critical one and is blind to untested critical paths.
  • 92.0% pass-rate against 74.0% risk-weighted coverage, an 18.0-point gap
  • 2 of 8 critical requirements undemonstrated, one with no test at all, invisible to the pass-rate
  • across 15 nights the pass-rate said go on 13 while the gate said no-go every night
129 tests100% coverage5 ADRs18 commitsaudit 9/10

capacity-overlap

Finds the over-allocation a tidy 100% sprint plan hides.

The impossibilityA resource plan summing to a tidy 100% per person per sprint cannot see over-allocation hidden by tasks overlapping across workstreams; the clash only appears at the day level.
  • 17.6 hidden person-days of over-allocation across 25 sprint-green pairs, affecting 16 people
  • 85.4% of the plan's over-allocation is invisible at sprint level
  • only 1.85 hidden person-days survive a quarter-FTE tolerance, the tool's own weakness stated first
124 tests100% coverage5 ADRs10 commitsaudit 9/10

status-audit

Recomputes a RAG status so a green-over-red rollup cannot hide.

The impossibilityA rolled-up program RAG status cannot be trusted by reading it; a human summary can be green outside and red underneath. Catching that needs an independent recomputation from objective signals.
  • 26 program-weeks reported Green while the signals computed Amber or Red, 16 of them Green over Red
  • 48 rollup integrity violations, 17 surviving the lenient majority rule
  • a robust floor of 26 that inflates to 63 only when the amber SPI line is tightened
